Iridium-oxide electrodes for artificial vision

Designing the electrochemical interface between chips and living systems. 設計晶片與生物體之間的電化學介面。

I work on iridium-oxide (IrOx) electrodes for implantable bioelectronics. At NAIST I lead artificial-vision research in Prof. Jun Ohta’s laboratory, building IrOx electrodes onto CMOS-driven retinal stimulators. My work runs from electrode chemistry and synchrotron X-ray spectroscopy to device integration, and on to recovering the iridium afterwards. I hold Ph.D. degrees from NAIST (2021) and National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University (2023) through a dual-degree program.

Research

研究

A retinal prosthesis talks to the eye through a few micrometers of electrode. My research follows that layer through its whole life: how it is made, how it is built into an implant, how it stimulates, and how its iridium is recovered.

Make · 製膜

Iridium-oxide films grown from solution

I grow IrOx films without vacuum tools: chemical bath deposition, Na-doped IrOx, bendable films from mild alkaline baths, temperature-controlled baths for pH sensing, and polydopamine–IrOx hybrid electrodeposition. Time-resolved synchrotron X-ray absorption spectroscopy follows how the films form in the bath.

J. Mater. Chem. C 2023 · Surf. Interfaces 2024 · Surf. Coat. Technol. 2025, 2026

Integrate · 整合

Electrodes on CMOS-based implants

I integrate IrOx electrodes with CMOS stimulator chips designed in our laboratory and assemble them into flexible retinal devices, including honeycomb-type and multi-module designs, using flip-chip bonding, micro-welding and Parylene C encapsulation. The devices are then evaluated in vitro and in vivo.

AIP Adv. 2021 · Micromachines 2025

Stimulate · 刺激

Charge into neurons, light into perception

Artificial vision is the main target: patterned pulses through the array stimulate the surviving retinal neurons and evoke phosphenes that build an image (Fig. 1). The same electrodes are being tested for pH and water-quality sensing and for electrical stimulation in agriculture, with collaborators in Japan and Taiwan.

Micromachines 2025 · Surf. Coat. Technol. 2025 (pH sensing)

Recover · 回收

Getting the iridium back

Iridium is one of the scarcest elements in the Earth’s crust, and the same metal is the catalyst that proton-exchange-membrane water electrolysers for green hydrogen depend on. I recover IrOx nanoparticles left over from deposition baths and turn them back into working stimulation electrodes, and I am extending this to regenerating iridium precursors from waste IrO2.

Ceram. Int. 2024 · ECS Meeting Abstracts 2026

Summer research internships, 2023–present

Each summer I host and supervise students from National Taipei University of Technology at NAIST through the Taiwan Ministry of Education overseas internship program, together with Prof. Po-Chun Chen and Prof. Shao-Sian Li. Interns present at international conferences (Best Poster Awards at SDSE 2024 and TACT 2025), and one co-first-authored a journal paper with me (Surf. Coat. Technol. 2026).

Research mentoring

I mentor a Ph.D. student at NAIST and graduate students in Prof. Pu-Wei Wu’s laboratory at National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University. The work of one master’s student I mentored led to an industry–academia collaboration with a Taiwanese manufacturer of DSA iridium-oxide electrodes.

Laboratory training and tools

I co-manage the shared tools in Prof. Ohta’s laboratory and train every new member on sputtering, flip-chip bonding, micro-welding, Parylene C coating, photolithography, potentiostats, thermal evaporation and a 532 nm laser system. I also built an AI-assisted online platform for instrument booking, SOPs and data analysis, and a Python-controlled pulse-electroplating system for IrOx that a Ph.D. student in the laboratory now uses.

Japan–Taiwan Young Researcher Workshop

Co-organizer of the 2024 and 2025 editions, funded by NAIST, and an invited speaker at both. The workshops brought together 21 researchers from 10 universities and companies in Japan and Taiwan, about 40–50 participants over the two editions.
